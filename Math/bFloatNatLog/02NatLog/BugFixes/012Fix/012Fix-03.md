# **2. The AGM Kernel We Ultimately Want**

The classical AGM iteration is:

$$ a_{0} = 1,\qquad g_{0} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{m}} $$

Then iterate:

$$ a_{n+1} = \frac{a_n + g_n}{2} $$

$$ g_{n+1} = \sqrt{a_n g_n} $$

As $n \to \infty$:

$$ \text{AGM}(1, 1/\sqrt{m}) = A $$

Then:

$$ \ln(m) = \frac{\pi}{2A} \left( 1 - \sum_{k=0}{\infty} 2{k+1} c_k^2 \right) $$

Where:

$$ c_k = a_k - g_k $$

This is the **Brent–Salamin AGM logarithm**.

### **Key point:**

Every step requires:

- $ \sqrt{x} $
- $ a_n g_n $
- $ \pi $ (you already have this)
- High‑precision division and multiplication (you already have these)

So the missing piece is **big.Float sqrt**.

# **3. Building Block #1 — A Robust big.Float Square Root**

The Newton iteration for square root is:

$$ x_{n+1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(x_n + \frac{S}{x_n}\right) $$

Where $S$ is the number whose square root we want.

### **Algorithm sketch (big.Float‑friendly)**

1. Normalize $S$ into mantissa × power‑of‑2
2. Choose an initial guess using float64
3. Iterate until convergence
4. Restore exponent
5. Round to requested precision

# **4. Building Block #2 — A big.Float Exponential Function**

You will eventually need:

$$ e^x $$

Even if AGM does not require it directly, your library will.

### **Best approach for big.Float exp**

Use:

$$ ex = 2{x / \ln(2)} $$

You already have:

- High‑precision $\ln(2)$
- High‑precision power‑of‑two scaling via `big.Float.SetMantExp`
- High‑precision multiplication and division

So the missing piece is:

### **Compute fractional power of 2:**

$$ 2y = e{y \ln(2)} $$

But that’s circular unless we use a **series expansion** for small $y$:

$$ ey = 1 + y + \frac{y2}{2!} + \frac{y^3}{3!} + \cdots $$

And reduce $x$ into:

$$ x = n \ln(2) + r,\quad |r| < 0.5 $$

Then:

$$ ex = 2n \cdot e^r $$

This is the standard MPFR approach.

```go

    aNext = (a + g) / 2
```
