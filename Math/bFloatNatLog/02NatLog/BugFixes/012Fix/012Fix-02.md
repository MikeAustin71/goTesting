### 1. Core AGM iteration recap

The arithmetic–geometric mean of $a_0,b_0>0$ is defined by the iteration

$$ \begin{aligned} a_{n+1} &= \frac{a_n + b_n}{2}, \ b_{n+1} &= \sqrt{a_n b_n}, \end{aligned} $$

and $a_n,b_n$ converge quadratically to a common limit

$$ \operatorname{AGM}(a_0,b_0) = \lim_{n\to\infty} a_n = \lim_{n\to\infty} b_n. $$

This is the “true AGM iteration” you’d want to see explicitly in `lnAGMMantissa`, not just a call to Taylor.

------

### 2. Where $\ln x$ enters the AGM world

AGM shows up naturally in complete elliptic integrals. A key identity is

$$ K(k) = \frac{\pi}{2,\operatorname{AGM}(1,\sqrt{1-k^2})}, $$

where $K(k)$ is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind.

Borwein/Brent/Bernstein–style algorithms then express $\ln x$ in terms of $K(k)$ (or related theta functions), and *that* is where AGM becomes a high‑precision log engine. Roughly:

- Use a transformation that maps $x$ to a parameter $k$ (or $q$) in an elliptic/theta setting.
- Evaluate $K(k)$ (or theta functions) via AGM.
- Recover $\ln x$ from $K(k)$ and the chosen transformation.

So a “true AGM log” is really: **range reduction → elliptic/theta transform → AGM loop → back‑transform to $\ln x$**.

### 3. Sketch A: Bernstein‑style AGM log (interval viewpoint)

Bernstein’s paper gives a very clean conceptual picture: maintain an interval $[L_n,U_n]$ that traps $\log x$, and update it using an AGM‑like transform on $x$.

High‑level idea:

1. **Range reduction**

   - Write $x = m \cdot 2^k$ with $m \in [1,2)$ (or a tighter window like $[1,1.5]$).
   - Then $\ln x = \ln m + k \ln 2$.
   - Your existing dispatcher already does something like this; `lnAGMMantissa` is the “$\ln m$” piece.

2. **AGM‑driven transform on $m$**

   You define a transform $T$ on $m$ such that:

   - $m_{n+1} = T(m_n)$,
   - $m_n \to 1$ rapidly,
   - and you can track $\ln m$ as a sum of small corrections at each step.

   Conceptually, you want something like

   $$ \ln m = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \Delta_n, $$

   where each $\Delta_n$ is computed from $(a_n,b_n)$ in an AGM iteration. Bernstein’s actual construction is more subtle (it uses complete elliptic integrals and interval arithmetic), but the pattern is:

   - Initialize $a_0,b_0$ from $m$ (for example, $a_0=1$, $b_0=f(m)$ for some function $f$).
   - Iterate AGM: $$ a_{n+1} = \frac{a_n + b_n}{2},\quad b_{n+1} = \sqrt{a_n b_n}. $$
   - At each step, update an accumulator that converges to $\ln m$.

### 4. Sketch B: Borwein–theta approach (conceptual only)
